## 0. The two answers
- **After 50+ levels of Doom the last act has to be MORE of that, not less.** Act D is four fight maps and a boss. The strangeness is dressing and cadence, short and earned: a dive down a shaft, a stealth map that ends in a brawl, a sprint along a collapse, a nine-wave stand, a boss. Every one of them is built on shooting, with the arsenal the player has carried for 60 levels (the mortar and the line-thrower finally matter: section 4). The only quiet in the act is the quiet AFTER the boss, and it is about four minutes long (C2M30).
- **There is no weird gadget.** Campaign 1's Lighthouse Cellar (C1E1S01) has the beacon note: "It is a counter-tone: a sound turned into light. Keep it lit, and it holds the tone back from the harbour." and Ines: "Thank you, Dad. I will keep it lit." The lamp IS the counter-tone, the player has carried a lantern since Act B, and the ending is her keeping a promise made in the first secret of the game. The player who found that secret gets the callback; the player who did not still gets a lamp, a Gate and a woman who stays.

## 1. What the act says (facts the text may use; to be merged into LORE.md as section 2d when the act is built)
- **The Root Bell is the deep stone's last voice,** hung at the bottom of the world in a hall that is the inside of a bell. The Songkeepers ring it by hand to keep the Gate open. **A bell can be made to stop; this one cannot be broken** (LORE section 2): the fight ends with it hanging silent and whole.
- **The beat is gone.** The Hive Cantor's death (M24) stopped the pulse the whole of Act C ran on. Act D is quiet because of it: the lights do not swell, the low pulse under the ambience is gone. What is left is the Songkeepers' last guard at the root and the stone, which answers the loss by cracking (M27).
- **The lamp holds sound back** (LORE, revised). Where a map says so (`lampHolds`), a lit lantern cuts the force of a sound that reaches the player: a pulse ring (the Cantors', a Resonant's, the Bell's) hurts a lit lamp's bearer much less. The plain hand lantern cuts a ring to 0.6; Ines's own lamp from the secret C2S02 to 0.4. The trade is the one Act B taught: eyes follow light (Gaunts, Gills and Zealots see a lit lamp from 24 m) and oil is finite (the lamp in C2M30 is eternal). That is the whole mechanic: nothing new to learn but a rule about a key the player already uses, told once, in M25, at the moment a ring comes.
- **The ninth stroke (M28).** LORE says Ines named the world after a bell on the ninth stroke of the tide table (the north pier's fog-bell, "Anselm"). The Last Tide is that bell, copied in the Hive's echo of the harbour; the stand lasts nine strokes.
- **A door is held from one side or the other** (the Tide-Warden of Pier Nine, C1E4S01). The Gate is a door that is also a bell, held open by the Root Bell. The Songkeepers cannot ring a lit lamp: **a lamp kept lit at the Gate holds it shut.** Somebody has to keep it lit. That is what a Tide-Warden is for.
- **Voices in Act D:** the Silent (hold-outs, M25 and M26: each refuses one thing, as in M22); Ines to a set that does not answer; HULL. SIGNAL HOUSE speaks once more, at the last (M30); it has not spoken since M23.

## 4. THE ROOT BELL (the final boss, `ENEMIES.rootbell`): a shooting fight inside a bell
**The arena.** The INSIDE OF A BELL: a round chamber 44 m across with a domed roof 17 m up hung with black roots, three tiers of stone seating round the floor at +1, +2, +3 m (the amphitheatre kit, as C2M16 and C2M24), eight root pillars on the tiers for cover and for steps (a ring passes over anyone a metre above it). Dark but for the lamp, a copper glow from the roots and the boss's own light. The map gives every weapon and the ammunition boxes stay (a boss map). A heal station and an ammunition cache on the top tier.
**The boss is the CLAPPER**, the Bell's heart: a 3.6 m mass of bronze and black root hung from the dome's centre on a chain, 2400 hit points (Easy 0.75). It swings across the arena on a pendulum (MOTION: a pure clock, period 6 s, the line turns 45 degrees at each phase): a MOVING TARGET the player must lead (the flare cannon, the rockets, the mortar's arc: skill shots; hitscan weapons hit it when it passes; the harpoon pins its swing). At each end of its swing it strikes the wall and a RING of sound rolls across the floor (the ring passes over anyone a metre above it: stand on a tier or a pillar's step; a lit lantern cuts it to 0.6, Ines's own lamp to 0.4). Under its path at the bottom of the swing it crushes (a shadow and a glow tell it a second ahead: the falling loads' telegraph).
- **Phase 1, THE TOLL.** Four ROOT ANCHORS (nodes, 350 hit points each, on four of the pillars) pin the chain: while any stands the clapper takes 30% of a hit. Shoot them from cover. Each anchor cut shortens the chain: the swing gets lower and wider (more dangerous, a bigger target). The Songkeepers' guard (REVISED PT-044, the owner on the Preview: "a lot of mobs for a test boss": two Zealots and two Choristers with the bell, two Zealots with the fall; an Acolyte or more only if the fight is judged too easy) comes in two waves.
- **Phase 2, THE DROP.** The last anchor goes and the clapper falls to the floor and becomes a ground boss: it CHARGES across the arena in straight lines after a one-second tell (the Warden-Graft's charge machinery, which exists: it stuns itself for three seconds on a pillar or the wall: bait it into a pillar, unload into it), rooted spikes strike up where the player stands (telegraphed a second ahead), and the guard's second wave (two Zealots) fights in the gaps. The tiers are a stair all the way round (a half-step at every radius): a player who goes down can always climb back.
- **Phase 3, THE LAST NOTE** (under 30%). The lights fall: only the lamp and the boss's glow are left; the Bell's note rolls the floor every three seconds (rings), the charge is faster, and the clapper's heart cracks open: its glow is the target. This is where the player's arsenal is spent: the MORTAR dropped in a high arc onto it (the weapon the vision named the "Bell-breaker"; a mortar shell that lands on the heart does triple), the line-thrower's rockets, a point-blank scattergun, the chainsaw. The final blow is whatever the player likes.
- **The end of the fight.** At zero it does NOT break. The charge dies mid-line, the clapper swings once more and hangs, the roots draw back from the walls, and **every sound in the game cuts out** (the score, the ambience, the guns, the player's own steps: a held second of nothing, then a single footstep) and the exit stair lights. That is the strange beat, and it is a reward the fight has paid for.
- **Why this works as a boss after Doom:** a big thing with a health bar, a moving target, cover to use, charges to dodge and bait, adds, a final burst, and every weapon in the player's hands. The lamp is a trade-off knob (cut the rings, but be seen) the player is already comfortable with. Nothing has to be explained mid-fight.
- **Fallback if it is not good in the Preview (concept B):** the Bell hangs whole above a ring arena; eight clapper-weights on chains round the rim are the anchors; its heart is shot through the mouth (mortar and carbine from the tiers); the rings and the guard are the same. Less dynamic, easier to tune; same ending.
- **Rig and look:** a clapper: a pear of bronze with a flared lip, wrapped in root, a chain of black links, a heart that glows through a split; nothing else in the game hangs, so the silhouette guard is easy. Eight sounds (the swing's creak, the strike, the ring, the charge, the root, the crack, the silence).

## 5. THE MAPS

### C2M25 Root Bell Descent (SET-PIECE, dark, threat 6; 80 x 80; about 50 enemies; par 540)
- **Figure and section.** A WELL: a round shaft 28 cells across with a void in the middle, six ring LANDINGS dropping round it (about 5 m a landing, 30 m in all: E13), each landing a half or three-quarter ring ledge with a roster; one-way: vents (the existing launch plates) throw the player across the void to the next landing, so the fall is seen: the whole shaft, the glow below (`sky_root_below`), the roots of the wall passing. Walls of black root; copper lamps hung on the landings; dark but for them and the lantern; a roof only at the top. A void under a sky: under 40% roofed: SET-PIECE.
- **Verb and new thing.** Fall on purpose, and fight on each ledge. THE LAMP HOLDS SOUND BACK, told at the niche on the third landing (the Silent's cache: oil, mortar ammunition and one plain line: "A lit lamp holds the sound back. Keep it lit when the rings come."), and used at once: the fourth and fifth landings each have a Resonant pair (the Act C callback) whose rings the lamp cuts.
- **Roster.** Choristers 8 (shooting at you while you cross the void), Gills 8 (the shaft is theirs), Zealots 8, Tollbearers 8, Gaunts 6, Bellhands 3, Bulwarks 3, Acolytes 2, Resonants 2, Sextons 2.
- **Way on.** Each landing's vent is its gate and is lit; the last landing's door opens on the exit (a lever, lit, named). **Secret:** a ledge under the sixth landing (a vent from the fifth that is not lit like the others) with a cache and a Silent's cairn.
- **Text.** The Silent's note at the niche (the lamp, once); Ines on the depth, in a number; HULL on the vents. Nothing about the Root Bell yet.

### C2M26 Silent Roots (MIXED, dark, threat 6; 100 x 100; about 43 enemies; par 600)
- **Figure and section.** A FOREST OF ROOT COLUMNS: a low cave of pillars at irregular spacing (6 to 10 m) with open lanes between them, three hold-out chambers (the Silent's) off a central hub, each behind a root gate, the Songkeepers' refectory at the far end. Heights: the hub at +0, chambers at +1.5 to +3 m, a sunk refectory -2 m; lids 3 to 8 m (a cave). `atmosphere.silence` (no score; the sound of the floor, the sleepers' breath), `dark: true` with a lantern: Gaunts, Gills and Zealots follow EYES (6 m dark, 24 lit), Tollbearers and Bellhands follow the HUSH (ordinary range).
- **Verb and new thing.** Sneak, then fight. The noise tell (E9) puts QUIET or LOUD and a radius on the HUD and a ring on the automap; a loud weapon wakes what stands within twice its radius at that moment; the arc lamp, melee and the fork are quiet. Light is the second sense: the lantern lit is seen from 24 m. Sleepers are killed quietly or passed; each chamber holds a Silent who refuses one thing and says where the sleepers are (the Silent GUIDE by what they say, not by following).
- **Roster.** Sleepers: Zealots 8, Gaunts 10, Tollbearers 6 (Hush-driven: eyes do not matter), Gills 4; the refectory brawl: Bulwarks 3, an Acolyte with a ward, Choristers 4, Bellhands 4, Sextons 2, a Weeping Mother 1.
- **Way on.** Three root gates, one lever in each chamber (a Silent's latch: a candle beside it, named: "the east gate", "the north gate", "the west gate"; in ANY order); the refectory gate opens when all three are thrown (`['msg:r1', 'msg:r2', 'msg:r3']`), and the exit is behind the refectory. Quiet play is rewarded by a cache in each chamber and fewer pre-woken enemies in the brawl; loud play is allowed and harder.
- **Beats.** 1 find the first chamber; 2 all three gates; 3 the refectory; 4 the exit. **Secret:** a hollow root column with a Songkeeper's reed and a tin.
- **Bot watch-outs.** The route bot keeps to the quiet weapon until the brawl (a route rule); the passive probe must still die or end at a locked exit; the robustness run must still finish.

### C2M27 Fault Line (SET-PIECE, threat 7; 128 x 60; about 43 enemies; par 480)
- **Figure and section.** A RIFT: a crack running west to east across the whole map, 10 m wide and open to a black sky; a road along its north lip and a second on its south, joined by seven bridges that are broken or sound as you reach them; heights from +4 m on the lips down to a shelf at +0; the world answering the loss of the beat.
- **Verb and new thing.** Run the line. A FRONT of falling stones starts at the west end at a time and advances along the rift at about a metre a second (the falling loads of Episode 4, with a `start`), so a stretch it has passed is a kill zone; `tremor` shakes the view before each break; bridge decks SINK after you cross (the existing sectors, a metre and a half, with a ramp out on the far side so no pit is a trap; one-way). Fights on the run: you are ahead of the front, not in front of a quiet room.
- **Roster.** Gills 8, Zealots 8, Choristers 6, Tollbearers 8, Gaunts 6, Bellhands 2, Bulwarks 3, Sextons 1, a Weeping Mother 1.
- **Way on.** A lever on the last lip raises the exit bridge, lit by the rift's glow and named from the first bridge ("the far lip"); the front's speed is tuned so the perfect bot always arrives with a margin and the passive runner is caught.
- **Beats.** 1 the first bridge; 2 the middle stretch; 3 the far lip; 4 the exit. **Secret:** a shelf at the bottom of the rift reached by a sunk deck's ramp (the one pit that is a choice).
- **Text.** HULL on what the stone does; Ines on the cold.

### C2M28 Last Tide (SET-PIECE, threat 10; 90 x 90; waves; par 600)
- **Figure and section.** A ROUND DOCK, the harbour's echo: a ring quay in three terraces round a basin, the Tide bell on a mole at the centre (the copy of the north pier's fog-bell, "Anselm"), a gate on the north quay; terraces at +0, +1.5, +3 m; walls only on the outside; open sky.
- **Verb and new thing.** Hold. The stand lasts NINE STROKES of the bell: the first eight are tolled by the dock itself (a stroke every 50 s after a first quiet minute, a toll from a trigger action) and the ninth is the player's own, on the rope at the mole: a wave comes with each tolled stroke from the four gates of the dock (the ninth wave, the last squad, comes when the eighth is dead); THE TIDE (E12) floods the lowest terrace at the third stroke and the middle one at the sixth, so the standing room shrinks and what swims (Gills) gains. Drop-fed ammunition, a few caches on the highest terrace.
- **Waves (spawns on `time:` triggers: Act A's M07 and Episode 3 already do this).** 1 Gaunts and Tollbearers; 2 Bellhands and Choristers; 3 Zealots (the tide at the third stroke); 4 Bulwarks and an Acolyte; 5 Resonants and a Weeping Mother; 6 Gills and Choristers (the tide at the sixth); 7 a mixed push; 8 the elites together; 9 the last squad (its death opens the mole). Total about 90 across the nine.
- **Way on.** The mole is shut by a gate that opens when the last wave is dead (a trigger on `dead:last`); on the mole the BELL ROPE (a lever, lit, named by the objective from the start: "ring the ninth stroke") is the player's ninth stroke, and ringing it opens the exit gate on the north quay (`msg:bell`). A passive runner dies before it, and a fighter in invulnerability cannot walk through a gate that is shut.
- **Beats.** 1 prepare (the first minute: the caches); 2 hold to the third, sixth, ninth strokes; 3 the gate. **Secret:** a drowned stair below the basin that the tide makes passable.
- **Text.** The strokes are counted aloud by a plate; Ines says what the bell is, once, in a number. No speech. The perfect bot must take at least 25 on hard and the passive runner must die; the map is tuned by the waves' size, never by making the player stand in water.

### C2M30 The Unrung Gate (no combat, threat 0; a linear walk of about 120 m; par 240)
- **Figure and section.** A SHORT WALK: callbacks of the descent in reverse, each a small room with the one object that stands for it, in silence (`atmosphere.silence`; the lamp is the only light and is eternal in this map): the floor of the Bell with the roots drawn back, a stair up the shaft (a plate: the depth), the Hive (the organs still, the beat gone), the Deep's city (lamps out, a single bell), the steppe (a road under a low red sun), the beach (the arch in the surf). - **The last input.** At the Gate the objective says "light the lamp". The player presses the lamp key (L) and keeps it lit; the lamp's light spreads over the Gate's door, the bell on it goes quiet, and SIGNAL HOUSE's voice comes in from the other side with the one line it has left (a plain one: it has her on the set). Then the exit fires and `ENDINGS[2]` plays, then the tally, then the closing titles. If the player took the quay lamp (C2S02), the cards name it.
- **Roster.** None. **Secret:** a side room with the last page of Pellew's log (the sentence the Ossuary's last page left unfinished, finished: one plain line).
- **The ending (decision 6: Ines closes the Gate and stays).** The cards are in the voice of Campaign 1's (`ENDINGS[1]`): the narrator (one line each time), SIGNAL HOUSE, INES. The facts they must carry, in order: the Bell has stopped; the Gate is a bell and a lit lamp holds it shut; someone has to stay and keep it lit; she told her father she would ("I said I would keep it lit": the first secret of the game, answered); someone is listening on the other side. Written after the owner has played the descent.
