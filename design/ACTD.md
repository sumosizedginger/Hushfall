## 1. What the act says (facts the text may use; to be merged into LORE.md as section 2d when the act is built)
- **The Root Bell is the deep stone's last voice,** hung at the bottom of the world in a hall the size of a bell. The Songkeepers ring it by hand to keep the Gate open. **It can be quieted, not broken** (LORE section 2); nobody in the game says why; the player finds out by doing it.
- **The beat is gone.** The Hive Cantor's death (M24) stopped the pulse the whole of Act C ran on. Act D is quiet because of it: the lights do not swell, the low pulse under the ambience is gone. What is left is the Songkeepers' last guard at the root, and the stone itself, which answers the loss by cracking (M27).
- **The counter-tone.** The Silent do not sing; they hold a note that answers nothing. They filed a Songkeeper's own reed to the opposite note and left it for "the one who does not stop" (found in M25). It mutes what is held up by sound: a ward, a reed's shield, a note that keeps a door shut, a ring that is about to be struck. It has no ammunition: a held tool on its own key (`tone`), with a charge that drains while held and fills when it is not. LORE: the Hush works by making a listener answer nothing; **Ines is the one person who can hold a note that answers nothing**, because she has never answered the Hush.
- **The ninth stroke (M28).** LORE says Ines named the world after a bell on the ninth stroke of the tide table (the north pier's fog-bell, "Anselm"). The Last Tide is that bell, copied, in the Hive's echo of the harbour; the stand lasts nine strokes. A reader who wants the origin finds it in M01's log; nobody has to.
- **A door is held from one side or the other** (the Tide-Warden of Pier Nine, C1E4S01). The Gate is a door that is also a bell, held open by the Root Bell; the way to close it is to quiet the bell and then to hold the quiet; Ines does the second part herself, in M30, and stays.
- **Voices in Act D:** the Silent (hold-outs, M25 and M26: each refuses one thing, as in M22); Ines to a set that does not answer; HULL. SIGNAL HOUSE speaks once more, at the last (M30); it has not spoken since M23.

## 4. THE ROOT BELL (the act's boss, `ENEMIES.rootbell`)
A colossal bell hanging from the roots of the dome: a bronze mouth 14 m across, black roots wound round its shoulder, a clapper the size of a man hung inside that is its heart. It never moves; it is hit through its mouth (the hit volume is the lip and the heart, not the full bell, which is 14 m tall: the fairness test and the measure tool fit it). It is not a person and has no face, and its death is not a death: **it stops ringing, and the game's sound stops with it** (`silence`). Hit points 1800 across three phases. The silhouette is unlike any other kind (nothing else hangs).
- **Phase 1, THE STRIKE.** Eight giant clappers (`bellnode`, reskinned: the bell's own tongue-weights, sound-shielded) hang round the ring; the bell strikes on a bar and a RING of sound crosses the floor (a pulse ring: it passes over anyone a metre above it: the tiers of the arena are the answer, as in C2M16). The player cancels a clapper's shield with the emitter and breaks it; four of the eight end the phase. Beat-free: the Hive's beat is gone, so the strikes are on the BELL's own bar (every 5 s, a long wind-up the light tells).
- **Phase 2, THE ROOTS.** The arena floor wakes: roots strike up where the player stands (falling loads' machinery, a telegraph a second before, a hit of 30), in lines across the floor; the Songkeepers' last squad (Zealots, an Acolyte whose ward the emitter strips) fights between the lines. The bell's heart is exposed between strikes.
- **Phase 3, THE ANSWER.** The Bell sings its one long note (a bar that builds on the HUD: the player cannot ignore it) and the way through is the emitter held on the mouth: the player fills an UNRUNG bar by holding the tone on the bell, runs dry, gets behind cover while the charge refills and the note's rings come (two cycles). When the bar fills, the bell quiets: the screen cuts to silence, the Songkeepers' squad stops, and the exit opens.
- **Why the emitter is the tool, not the gun:** the Root Bell cannot be broken (the lore); a gun does the roots' work, the tone does the Bell's.

## 5. THE MAPS

### C2M25 Root Bell Descent (SET-PIECE, threat 6; 80 x 80; about 50 enemies; par 540)
- **Figure and section.** A WELL: a round shaft 28 cells across with a void in the middle, six ring LANDINGS dropping round it (about 5 m a landing, 30 m in all: E13), each landing a half or three-quarter ring ledge with a roster; one-way: vents (the existing launch plates) throw the player across the void to the next landing, so the fall is seen: the whole shaft, the glow below (`sky_root_below`), the roots of the wall passing. Walls of black root; copper light from small lamps hung on the landings; a roof only at the top. A void under a sky: under 40% roofed: SET-PIECE.
- **Verb and new thing.** Fall on purpose. THE COUNTER-TONE EMITTER is found on the third landing in a niche left by the Silent (lit, named, on the lane: a note filed to the opposite pitch, with a plain line); its first use is the TONELOCK door on the fourth landing (a small door shut by a note: hold the tone on it); then a shielded Reed on the fifth, so the player uses it on an enemy before the next map relies on it. The tone is TOLD by the line at the niche, the prompt at the door and a HUD gauge.
- **Roster.** Choristers 8 (shooting at you while you cross the void), Gills 8 (the shaft is theirs), Zealots 8, Tollbearers 8, Gaunts 6, Bellhands 3, Bulwarks 3, Acolytes 2 (the first with a ward to strip), Reeds 2 (shielded), Sextons 2.
- **Way on.** Each landing's vent is its gate and is lit; the last landing's door (the tonelock) opens on the exit. **Secret:** a ledge under the sixth landing (a vent from the fifth that is not lit like the others) with a cache and a Silent's cairn.
- **Text.** The Silent's note at the niche (one fact: what the tone is for, said once); Ines on the depth, in a number. HULL on the door. Nothing about the Root Bell yet.

### C2M26 Silent Roots (MIXED, dark, threat 6; 100 x 100; about 43 enemies; par 600)
- **Figure and section.** A FOREST OF ROOT COLUMNS: a low cave of pillars at irregular spacing (6 to 10 m) with open lanes between them, three hold-out chambers (the Silent's) off a central hub, each behind a root gate, the Songkeepers' refectory at the far end. Heights: the hub at +0, chambers at +1.5 to +3 m, a sunk refectory -2 m; lids 3 to 8 m (a cave). `atmosphere.silence` (no score; the sound of the floor, the sleepers' breath), `dark: true` with a lantern: Gaunts, Gills and Zealots follow EYES (6 m dark, 24 lit), Tollbearers and Bellhands follow the HUSH (ordinary range). Light: a few copper lamps in the Silent's chambers; if the player took the lamp in C2S02 it never burns out.
- **Verb and new thing.** Sneak, then fight. The noise tell (E9) puts QUIET or LOUD and a radius on the HUD and a ring on the automap; a loud weapon wakes what stands within twice its radius at that moment; the arc lamp, melee, the fork and the tone are quiet. Sleepers are killed quietly or passed; each chamber holds a Silent who refuses one thing and says where the sleepers are (the Silent GUIDE by what they say, not by following).
- **Roster.** Sleepers: Zealots 8, Gaunts 10, Tollbearers 6 (Hush-driven: eyes do not matter), Gills 4; the refectory brawl: Bulwarks 3, an Acolyte with a ward (the tone strips it: the second use of E10), Choristers 4, Bellhands 4, Sextons 2, a Weeping Mother 1.
- **Way on.** Three root gates, one lever in each chamber (a Silent's latch: a candle beside it, named by the objective: "the east gate", "the north gate", "the west gate"; in ANY order); the refectory gate opens when all three are thrown (`['msg:r1', 'msg:r2', 'msg:r3']`), and the exit is behind the refectory. Quiet play is rewarded by a cache in each chamber and fewer pre-woken enemies in the brawl; loud play is allowed and harder.
- **Beats.** 1 find the first chamber; 2 all three gates; 3 the refectory; 4 the exit. **Secret:** a hollow root column with a Songkeeper's reed and a tin.
- **Bot watch-outs.** The route bot must keep to the quiet weapon until the brawl (a route rule), the passive probe must still die or end at a locked exit, and the robustness run (aim error, less ammunition) must still finish.

### C2M27 Fault Line (SET-PIECE, threat 7; 128 x 60; about 43 enemies; par 480)
- **Figure and section.** A RIFT: a crack running west to east across the whole map, 10 m wide and open to a black sky; a road along its north lip and a second on its south, joined by seven bridges that are broken or sound as you reach them; heights from +4 m on the lips down to a shelf at +0; the world answering the loss of the beat.
- **Verb and new thing.** Run the line. A FRONT of falling stones starts at the west end at a time and advances along the rift at about a metre a second (the falling loads of Episode 4, with a `start`, so the front approaches from behind and a stretch it has passed is a kill zone); `tremor` shakes the view before each break; bridge decks SINK after you cross (the existing sectors, a metre and a half, with a ramp out on the far side so no pit is a trap; one-way). Fights on the run: you are ahead of the front, not in front of a quiet room.
- **Roster.** Gills 8 (the rift is theirs), Zealots 8, Choristers 6, Tollbearers 8, Gaunts 6, Bellhands 2, Bulwarks 3, Sextons 1, a Weeping Mother 1.
- **Way on.** A lever on the last lip raises the exit bridge (`msg:`), lit by the rift's own glow and named from the first bridge ("the far lip"); the front's speed is tuned so the perfect bot always arrives with a margin and the passive runner is caught.
- **Beats.** 1 the first bridge; 2 the middle stretch; 3 the far lip; 4 the exit. **Secret:** a shelf at the bottom of the rift reached by a sunk deck's ramp (the one pit that is a choice).
- **Text.** HULL on what the stone does; Ines on the cold; nothing else (the map is a sprint).

### C2M28 Last Tide (SET-PIECE, threat 10; 90 x 90; waves; par 600)
- **Figure and section.** A ROUND DOCK, the harbour's echo: a ring quay in three terraces round a basin, the Tide bell on a mole at the centre (the copy of the north pier's fog-bell, "Anselm"), a gate on the north quay; terraces at +0, +1.5, +3 m; walls only on the outside; open sky.
- **Verb and new thing.** Hold. The stand lasts NINE STROKES of the bell: the first eight are tolled by the dock itself (a stroke every 50 s after a first quiet minute, a toll from a trigger action) and the ninth is the player's own, on the rope at the mole: a wave comes with each tolled stroke from the four gates of the dock (the ninth wave, the last squad, comes when the eighth is dead); THE TIDE (E12) floods the lowest terrace at the third stroke and the middle one at the sixth, so the standing room shrinks and what swims (Gills) gains. Drop-fed ammunition (the dead feed you), a few caches on the highest terrace, the emitter available for the Acolytes' wards. SET-PIECE.
- **Waves (the spawns on `time:` triggers: Act A's M07 and Episode 3 already do this).** 1 Gaunts and Tollbearers; 2 Bellhands and Choristers; 3 Zealots (the tide at the third stroke); 4 Bulwarks and an Acolyte; 5 Resonants and a Weeping Mother; 6 Gills and Choristers (the tide at the sixth); 7 a mixed push; 8 the elites together; 9 the last squad (its death opens the mole). Total about 90 across the nine.
- **Way on.** The mole is shut by a gate that opens when the last wave is dead (a trigger on `dead:last`); on the mole the BELL ROPE (a lever, lit, named by the objective from the start: "ring the ninth stroke") is the player's ninth stroke, and ringing it opens the exit gate on the north quay (`msg:bell`). A passive runner dies before it, and a fighter in invulnerability cannot walk through a gate that is shut. The rope cannot be reached early, so the ninth stroke is never rung out of order.
- **Beats.** 1 prepare (the first minute: the caches); 2 hold to the third, sixth, ninth strokes; 3 the gate. **Secret:** a drowned stair below the basin that the tide makes passable.
- **Text.** The strokes are counted aloud by a plate ("the third stroke"); Ines says what the bell is, once, in a number (the ninth stroke of the tide table). No speech.
- **The danger of this map for the bots:** the perfect bot must take at least 25 on hard and the passive runner must die; the map is tuned by the waves' size, never by making the player stand in water.

### C2M29 The Root Bell (SET-PIECE, boss, threat 10; 96 x 96; boss map: keeps every ammunition box; par 720)
- **Figure and section.** The INSIDE OF THE BELL: a domed ring arena (the amphitheatre kit: a floor ring, a ring of three tiers at +1, +2, +3 m, a dome of 35 units overhead hung with roots), the Bell hung over the centre, its clapper at head height inside its mouth; eight clapper-weights round the ring; the Songkeepers' last squad in the tiers.

- **Beats.** 1 the clappers; 2 the roots; 3 the answer; 4 the stair. **Secret:** a root hollow behind a pillar with a cache.
- **Text.** None. The Bell says nothing; the Songkeepers' squad says one line on first sight.

### C2M30 The Unrung Gate (no combat, threat 0; a linear walk of about 160 m; par 300)
- **Figure and section.** A LONG WALK: callbacks of the descent in reverse, each a small room with the one object that stands for it, in silence (`atmosphere.silence` all through; the lamp is the only light and is eternal in this map whatever the player took): the floor of the Bell (the roots drawn back), the shaft (a stair going up, a plate: the depth), the Hive (the organs still, the beat gone, the lights dark), the Deep's city (lamps out, the market empty, a single bell), the steppe (a road under a low red sun), the beach (the arch in the surf). At the top the GATE seen from the Vael side: a door that is also a bell.
- **The last input.** At the Gate the player holds the tone (a held key: the same one they have used since M25) for twenty seconds; Ines holds it; SIGNAL HOUSE's voice swells in from the other side and speaks (the one line it has left, a plain one: it has her on the set). Releasing the tone stalls the beat of the epilogue until it is held again. When it is done the exit fires and `ENDINGS[2]` plays, then the tally, then the closing cards and titles.
- **Roster.** None. **Secret:** a side room with the last page of Pellew's log (the sentence the Ossuary's last page left unfinished, finished: one plain line).
- **The ending (decision 6: Ines closes the Gate and stays).** The cards are in the voice of Campaign 1's (`ENDINGS[1]`): the narrator (one line each time), SIGNAL HOUSE, INES. The facts they must carry, in order: the Bell is quiet and the Gate is shutting; it shuts from the Vael side because that is the only side it can be held from; the lamp stays lit; someone is listening on the other side; she stays. Written after the owner has played the descent: it is the story's heart and the least certain text (CAMPAIGN2.md risk).
