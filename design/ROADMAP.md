## 1. The last boss
**Yes: the ROOT BELL (C2M29)**, the final boss of the whole game (`design/ACTD.md` section 4). It is a SHOOTING fight inside the bell: the boss is the Bell's heart, a 3.6 m clapper that swings on a pendulum across the arena (a moving target to lead), held by four root anchors you shoot from cover; cut them and it falls and CHARGES (bait it into a pillar and unload); in the last phase the lights fall, the Bell's note rolls rings across the floor, its heart cracks open, and the whole arsenal goes into it (the mortar, the rockets, the scattergun, the chainsaw). The Songkeepers' last guard and a pair of Resonants fight in the gaps. A lit lantern cuts the rings (the lamp is the counter-tone: Campaign 1's first secret already says so; there is no new gadget). When it falls it does not break: it hangs silent and the game's sound cuts out for a held second. C2M30 is then a four-minute walk up to the Gate and one last input: Ines lights the lamp and stays to keep it lit, as she told her father she would. Campaign 2's bosses in order: the Sub-Cantor (C2M16, built and played), the Hive Cantor (C2M24: a read-the-beat fight where killing the choir opens it), the Root Bell (C2M29). Campaign 1's four are built. Neither of the last two Campaign 2 bosses exists yet.

## 4. The unattended run: the contract (what "set you free" means, so nobody is surprised)
- **What I do without asking:** S0 drop A first and then STOP FOR THE OWNER'S PLAY (that stop is by design: the new creatures and both boss fights are the things the owner asked to see before anything is built on them), then drop B, then, on the owner's word, S1 to S4 in order; every design decision (the owner's standing words: "ALL DECISIONS ARE YOURS"; they overrule afterwards); fixes to my own mistakes; an update of `PRODUCTION_LOG.md` at every stage boundary (a fresh session resumes from the file, not from me); a memory note per stage.
- **How I commit:** a local commit per map and per engine piece (any one can be reverted), tests green before each commit. **I do not push** unless the owner says push (pushing publishes, and the outside auditors read the repository). I will say when a push is worth making: after the Preview, after S1, after S2, and at RC1.
- **Chrome jobs (bakes, stills, the browser checks, the audio QA):** headless, idle priority, two cores, ONE at a time, never the owner's mouse (the machine-load rule in `AGENTS.md` stands: the owner's mouse stopped reaching the screen while such jobs ran, the cause was never proven). The owner's "go" is the word that the two-core idle jobs may run in the background; if they say "the machine is busy" I pause the Chrome jobs and carry on in node. The long jobs (the whole browser check, the evidence regeneration, the audio QA) are scheduled in S3, together.
- **Stop conditions (I stop and report, I do not push through):** a test or gate that can only be passed by weakening it (fix the game, not a valid test); a plan assumption that proves false (as the 17 m height cap did: I correct the plan in writing and take the fallback it names); a change that would move a shipped map (the fingerprints say so); anything destructive or outward (a push, a delete, an account, a deploy).
- **What I will NOT do on my own:** push; enable hosting; touch the owner's untracked files (the audit notes, `Your Mission.md`, `review/audit-*`); award COMPLETE; kill processes by image name; take the mouse.
- **What the run cannot do, and the owner must:** play it. The gates prove what they can (a bot finishes every route on three difficulties, a passive runner never gets through, an invulnerable fighter cannot walk to a locked exit, ammunition slack, robustness, no trap, the findability lint) and the real page is probed for what is DRAWN against the sim; but nobody has felt the carriers, the Resonant's ring, the lamp cutting a ring, the Hive Cantor's tiers, the clapper or the ninth stroke. The Preview exists so that is found out BEFORE fifteen maps are built on it.
- **Reporting:** a short note at each stage boundary in the conversation; at the end of S4 one report (what exists, what is proven, what is not, where to start playing: the DEV level picker opens any map).

## 5. Done, at each size
- **A map is done when:** `npm run verify-map -- <ID>` passes every node gate; its vantage stills have been looked at and show its figure and its look; its text passes the mechanical text rules; its gate object passes the findability lint; the route stories show every message delivered; its real-game run (S3) is current. It then reads AGENT_VERIFIED. COMPLETE is the owner's.
- **An act is done when** every map is, its creatures are in the Preview and under 0.5 silhouette overlap with every other kind, its tranche's fingerprints are identical, and `PRODUCTION_LOG.md` says so.
- **The game is done (RC1)** when S3 and S4's exits hold and the owner has the pack in hand.

## 7. What I most expect to go wrong, and the answer already written down
1. **Moving cars** (the one hard engine piece): the Preview's car yard says early; the fallback keeps M20's plan with conveyors and lifts.
2. **The taller format** touches every map's format: the fingerprints are the proof; the fallback is M25 at 17 m.
3. **The two boss fights are layered:** the bot gates prove they can be won and cannot be skipped, not that they are fun; the Preview is where the owner finds out, and the plans name the cut lines (tier strikes in M24; the pendulum in M29, with concept B).
4. **Look at scale:** 15 more maps risk the sameness the audits found. The shape contract, the roster-variety rule, the one-new-thing rule and a look of its own per map are the defence; the Preview's look wing is the first check.
5. **The ending's text** is written last and is the least certain; it is revised after the owner has played the descent.
6. **Evidence cost:** one regeneration for the whole campaign in S3, from one clean commit.
