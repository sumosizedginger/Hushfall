## 1. The last boss
**Yes: the ROOT BELL (C2M29)**, the final boss of the whole game, planned in `design/ACTD.md` sections 4 and 5. A colossal bell hung from the roots of the dome at the bottom of the world; three phases (the Strike: eight clapper-weights and a ring of sound; the Roots: the floor wakes; the Answer: the Bell's one long note, cancelled by holding the counter-tone on its mouth). It is not broken, it is **quieted**: the score and the world's sound cut out with it. C2M30 is the walk back up and the held tone, with no combat. Campaign 2's bosses in order: the Sub-Cantor (C2M16, built and played), the Hive Cantor (C2M24, a read-the-beat fight where killing the choir opens it), the Root Bell (C2M29). Campaign 1's four are built (the Cantor, the Graft-Mother, the Grand Cantor, the Gate Warden). Nothing of the last two bosses of Campaign 2 is built yet.

## 4. The unattended run: the contract (what "set you free" means, so nobody is surprised)
- **What I do without asking:** S1 to S6 in order, every design decision (the owner's standing words: "ALL DECISIONS ARE YOURS"; they overrule afterwards), every map and creature and engine piece in the two plans, fixes to my own mistakes, an update of `PRODUCTION_LOG.md` at every stage boundary (so a fresh session resumes from the file, not from me), a memory note per act.
- **How I commit:** a local commit per map and per engine piece (so any one can be reverted), tests green before each commit. **I do not push** unless the owner says push (pushing publishes: the outside auditors read the repository, so a push is the owner's call; I will say when a push is worth making: after S2, after S4, and at RC1).
- **Chrome jobs (bakes, stills, the browser checks, the audio QA):** headless, idle priority, two cores, ONE at a time, never the owner's mouse (the machine-load rule in `AGENTS.md` stands: the owner's mouse stopped reaching the screen while such jobs ran, the cause was never proven). The owner's "go" for this run is the word that the two-core idle jobs may run in the background; if they say "the machine is busy" I pause the Chrome jobs and carry on with node-only work, and resume when they say. The long jobs (the whole browser check, the evidence regeneration, the audio QA) are scheduled in S5, together, not scattered.
- **Stop conditions (I stop and report, I do not push through):** a test or gate that can only be passed by weakening it (the project's rule: fix the game, not a valid test); a plan assumption that proves false (as the 17 m height cap did: I correct the plan in writing and take the fallback the plan names); a change that would move a shipped map (the fingerprints say so); anything destructive or outward (a push, a delete, an account, a deploy).
- **What I will NOT do on my own:** push; enable hosting; touch the owner's untracked files (the audit notes, `Your Mission.md`, `review/audit-*`); award COMPLETE; kill processes by image name; take the mouse.
- **What the run cannot do, and the owner must:** play it. Everything the gates can prove they prove (a bot finishes every route on three difficulties, a passive runner never gets through, an invulnerable fighter cannot walk to a locked exit, ammunition slack, robustness, no trap, the findability lint), and the real page is probed for what is DRAWN against the sim; but nobody has felt the carriers, the Resonant's ring, the emitter held in the hand, the Hive Cantor's tiers or the ninth stroke. S7 is where that is found, which is why S7 is the owner's and the first one after the build.
- **Reporting:** a short note at each stage boundary in the conversation; at the end of S6 one report (what exists, what is proven, what is not, where to start playing: the DEV level picker opens any map).

## 5. Done, at each size
- **A map is done when:** `npm run verify-map -- <ID>` passes every node gate; its vantage stills have been looked at and show its figure and its look; its text passes the mechanical text rules; its gate object passes the findability lint; the route stories show every message delivered; its real-game run (S5) is current. It then reads AGENT_VERIFIED. COMPLETE is the owner's.
- **An act is done when** every map is, the act's creatures are in the Range and under 0.5 silhouette overlap with every other kind, its tranche's fingerprints are identical, and `PRODUCTION_LOG.md` says so.
- **The game is done (RC1)** when S5 and S6's exits hold and the owner has the pack in hand.

## 7. What I most expect to go wrong, and the answer already written down
1. **Moving cars** (S1, the only hard engine piece): the Range yard says early; the fallback keeps M20's plan with conveyors and lifts.
2. **Act D's taller format** touches every map's format: the fingerprints are the proof; the fallback is M25 at 17 m.
3. **The Hive Cantor and the Root Bell are layered fights:** the bot gates prove they can be won and cannot be skipped, not that they are fun; the plans name the cut lines (tier strikes in M24, the second cycle in M29's last phase).
4. **Look at scale:** 15 more maps in the same engine risk the same sameness the audits found. The shape contract, the roster-variety rule, the one-new-thing rule and a look of its own per map are the defence; the audit pack makes it checkable.
5. **The ending's text** is written last and is the least certain; it is revised after the owner has played the descent.
6. **Evidence cost:** one regeneration for the whole campaign in S5 (not per act), from one clean commit.
