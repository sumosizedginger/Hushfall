# Performance and compatibility plan (M4 of the commercial workstream, written 2026-10-19; nothing here is measured on a machine other than the owner's)

## What is known
- The owner's machine (an RTX 3060 Ti class card) runs the game with real graphics at about 59 frames a second on the worst map found so far, Glass Steppe, after the enemy-navigation fix (PT-054: a distance-field cache that thrashed under hunters; 0.6 fps before, 59 after, measured by `HUNT=1 GPU=1 node tools/dev/probe-prof.mjs`). The owner confirmed "no stutter" on 2026-10-19.
- The game renders at a LOW INTERNAL WIDTH (320 to 800 pixels, `RESOLUTIONS` in `src/game/settings.js`, then upscaled), so fill rate is small by design: what costs time is the simulation (creatures, navigation), the number of draw calls and the number of objects whose matrix is recomputed every frame. The probes below measure exactly those.
- A production build is `dist/`: 165 files, 8.9 MB (6.0 MB of painted atlases, 3.0 MB of script, the maps inside it). `npm run check-dist` says it can be uploaded as it stands.
- Every browser run so far was Chromium, either on SwiftShader (software) or on the owner's one card. Nothing has run on an integrated GPU, a laptop, Firefox or Safari, and no resolution but the ones the harness picked.

## The minimum spec we are aiming at (a target, not a measurement)
An integrated GPU of about 2020 (Intel Iris Xe class) at the game's 480-pixel default internal width, 60 frames a second on every map and 30 at worst on the 81-creature stand (C2M28 Last Tide) and the largest maps (C2M26 Silent Roots at 84 x 82, C2M09 Sounding Deep Gate at 104 x 92). A browser from the last two years: Chromium and Firefox first, Safari after.

## How to measure it (each is ONE headless-Chrome job and needs the owner's named yes; the node ones need none)
- Node, any machine: `node tools/dev/sim-cost.mjs [--difficulty=hard] <ID> ...` (the simulation's milliseconds per tick over a whole route: the creatures-and-navigation share of a frame), `node tools/dev/nav-bench.mjs [--hunters=30] <ID> ...`, `node tools/dev/scene-census.mjs <ID> ...` (objects, draw calls, matrices that recompute).
- Browser, the owner's real card: `GPU=1 node tools/dev/probe-prof.mjs <outDir> <ID> ...` (`HUNT=1` is the worst case, `WAKE=1` wakes everyone, `FIRE=1` holds the trigger, `AUDIO=1` lets the audio run), `probe-perf-steady.mjs` (render time and draw calls per vantage and with each layer hidden), `probe-perf.mjs` (every frame of a whole route). Run them through `tools/dev/low.ps1` with absolute paths, one at a time.
- On OTHER machines (a tester's laptop, a borrowed one): the same probes need Node and the repository, which a tester will not have. The way is the opt-in play log of workstream I / M11: the build records its own frame times and the percentiles it saw and writes them to a file the player can send. IT IS BUILT (PT-071, R24): `src/game/playlog.js` + the Settings switch "Keep a play log on this device" (off unless turned on): one entry per attempt (map, difficulty, how it ended, seconds, damage, the frame times of that attempt as a 200-bin histogram with its 50th / 95th / 99th percentile and the render width, the browser and the graphics card's name), five optional questions after a map, and an Export / Copy / Clear. Nothing is ever sent (`tests/playlog.test.js` forbids any network call in it); a tester presses Export and sends the file. The frame percentiles are the real-hardware numbers this plan has been missing.

## The automatic quality fallback (BUILT, PT-071 R21; the second rung BUILT in FREEZE F11, 2026-10-25, not yet seen on a slow machine)
`src/game/quality.js` (`QualityGovernor`, pure: frame times in, a width out; `tests/quality.test.js`) and a Settings switch "Lower the render width by itself when the game runs slowly" (on by default). The INTERNAL WIDTH steps down one rung (800 -> 640 -> 480 -> 400 -> 320) when the TYPICAL frame (the 60th percentile of the last 240 samples, at least 150 of them) stays over 22 ms, never more than two rungs under the player's own choice, at most one step per three seconds; it steps back up after thirty seconds in which the typical frame is under 18.5 ms (a dead band above one 60 Hz frame, so a 60 Hz display counts as quiet), and the wait doubles (up to 8x) each time a rung fails again. Frames over 250 ms (a hidden tab, a pause) are not samples. The simulation runs on a fixed step and never reads the frame rate: only what is drawn changes.

What the real page showed (`tools/dev/probe-quality.mjs`: the real game in headless Chrome, the CPU throttled by the DevTools protocol; Chrome 154 on the owner's RTX 3060 Ti through ANGLE / D3D11 unless noted; 2026-10-20):
- C1E1M01 at width 640: held 640 at 6x CPU throttle, 95th percentile 16.8 ms. Nothing to fix, nothing changed.
- C2M26 Silent Roots at 640, 6x throttle: with the first version (the 95th percentile) it stepped 640 -> 480 -> 400 on 33 ms bursts that a smaller picture does not cure (the simulation, not the pixels); that is why the controller judges the typical frame. At 20x throttle, width 800: median 16.7 ms, 95th percentile 33 to 50 ms: it is CPU-bound, and correctly does not step down.
- C1E1M01 on the SOFTWARE renderer (SwiftShader, where the picture itself is the slow part), width 800: 800 -> 640 -> 480 and held at 33 ms frames, with and without the CPU throttle. The two-rung floor stopped it; the controller does what it is for.
Limits, said plainly: the governor cures only a slow PICTURE (fill rate, draw calls scaling with pixels). A machine whose cost is the simulation (many creatures on a slow CPU) is not helped by a smaller picture. THE SECOND RUNG (FREEZE F11, `QualityGovernor` `fx` 0 to 2, `GameView.setEffects`): when the width is already as low as the governor lets it go and the typical frame is still over 22 ms, the EFFECTS step down one level at a time: level 1 hides the creatures' contact shadows and the marks on the floors, level 2 also lights only the nearest four lamps (a one-time shader recompile, then a shorter light loop). They come back BEFORE the width does, each after the same thirty quiet seconds with the same backoff when a level fails again; a new width choice or turning the fallback off puts everything back, and a new map starts at the level the governor is at. Render only (the simulation never reads it); `tests/quality.test.js`. What it cannot do is also unchanged: it cures a slow picture, not a slow simulation. NOT SEEN on a slow machine: the numbers (22 ms, thirty seconds) are the first rung's, the two levels are a judgement of what costs most per pixel and per light; a tester's play log (the 99th percentile and the width it ended on) will say whether a third level is needed. No integrated GPU, laptop or Firefox / Safari run exists yet.

## What would change the plan
A real integrated-GPU number that is bad would move the work to draw-call merging (`src/render/merge.js`) and the creature rigs (a rig is many meshes: the kit merges the static parts of a joint, not the animated ones). A real number that is good means this file is done and the fallback stays a design.

## Window sizes (R21, PT-071, 2026-10-20)
`node tools/dev/probe-viewports.mjs <out.json>` (one headless-Chrome job) opens the title, pause, game-over, Record, credits and crash screens in the real page at 800x600, 1024x768, 1280x720, 1366x768, 1600x900, 1920x1080, 2560x1440, 3440x1440 and a portrait 768x1024, with every fold (settings, testers, credits and licences) open, and checks that nothing runs past a side, nothing sits off the top or bottom of a screen that cannot scroll, the page does not scroll sideways and the canvas covers the window. Result: 54 screens, 0 problems, 0 pointer-lock requests, 0 page errors. The dev-build level picker is skipped (it is not in the shipped game). Chrome only: Firefox and Safari are not installed here and have not been run.

## Soak and the simulation's own cost (R21, PT-071, 2026-10-20)
`node tools/dev/soak.mjs [--ticks=N] [ID ...]` plays a world for a long time with an immortal, restless player in a fight that never ends (everything asleep is woken every ten seconds) and compares the size of everything the world keeps between the first third and the last. 60000 ticks (16 minutes of play) on Marrow Quay, the Marrow Nave, the Pulse Foundry and the Last Tide: nothing keeps growing. The slowest single simulation step on this machine: 4.7 ms (Quay), 5.0 (Nave), 6.3 (Foundry) and 13.4 ms (Last Tide, 81 hunting creatures) of a 16.7 ms frame. THE LIMIT, plainly: the render-width fallback cannot help a machine whose cost is this step: on a CPU two or three times slower than this one the Last Tide's worst step would not fit in a frame, and that is the number a tester's play log (the 99th percentile frame) would show. `tests/soak.test.js` holds the cheap version (9000 ticks, three maps) in the suite.
