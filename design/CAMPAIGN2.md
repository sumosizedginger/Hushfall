# Campaign 2: The Long Descent (30 main maps and two secrets)

**PLAN ONLY. Nothing of Campaign 2 is built, no map, no creature, no engine step.** Written 2026-10-11 after the Campaign 1 ending beat (the owner: "we need a real ending beat for the campaign, and then lets plan campaign 2 when you are ready"). The owner's standing words on who decides: "you're the builder, you make the choice, I just make sure it looks and feels good", so the decisions below carry my pick and can be overruled. The plan follows the shape of `design/EPISODE4.md` (the owner liked how it ran: engine step first, tried in the Range, then the maps); the manifest (`CAMPAIGN_MANIFEST.json`, slots C2M01 to C2M30, C2S01, C2S02) already holds every map's name, thesis and narrative purpose and is kept as written.

## What Campaign 1 leaves us, and what the owner's play says
- 36 maps, 19 creature kinds (the nodes and the Warden's seals included) with four bosses (the Cantor, the Graft-Mother, the Grand Cantor, the Gate Warden), 15 weapons (ranged and melee), the Locker, drops, the Range. Everything runs on the same deterministic sim, so every new rule below is pure (a function of `w.time` or lazy state: a world that never uses it is byte-for-byte what it was, proved by `route-fingerprint.mjs check`).
- The owner played Episode 3 and 4 on Easy: difficulty reads Doom-like and intended, movement and feel liked ("The jump pads work great"), **"none of it really feels like a puzzle yet"** (Episode 4 answered with the Mirror Array and the Warden's mirrors: they have now played the Warden: "plays and looks good", and they shot the seals instead of using the mirrors, so a mechanic must be TOLD at the moment it matters: the Warden's start line and objective now say it), and one real stall (the Cold Dock lock: a mechanic whose state the player cannot read costs more than a hard fight).
- What the three outside audits and my own taste assessment found (PT-035): the Tollbearer and the Gaunt are 75% of Episodes 1 and 2 (a roster that varies matters more than another map); the four coat-wearers share one column (new creatures must be wide, low, tall or winged, not a fifth coat); Episode 2 is the flattest episode (every map gets the SHAPE CONTRACT from its first line, not a reshape afterwards).
- The ending beat exists (`src/game/ending.js`): a finale's exit shows an epilogue of cards over a painting and the tally of the whole campaign, then the intermission. `isFinale` already says C2M30 is the next finale; Campaign 2 only has to write `ENDINGS[2]`.

## The story spine (the builder's; every line can be overruled)
- **Where.** The Gate opens onto **Anselm-Nine** (the manifest's name), the Vael's own world: dying, and quiet. "The Vael world is dying and quiet" (M02): that is WHY they harvest minds: they can no longer sing. The Song is an economy (M04).
- **Who.** **Ines** goes alone: the radio ends at the Gate ("There will be no line down there. I will lose you." / "Stay on the air anyway. Somebody should be listening for me."). SIGNAL HOUSE is silent for most of the descent; its voice comes back once, in **M23 The Reprise** (the Song has reproduced the harbour, and the signal with it, wrong), and speaks once more in M30. Voices in the Deep are **the Tender** (the Vael grave-tender of the secret C2S01 and the first Vael who speaks to her: freed from the cage in M03) and **the Silent** (the dissenters of M11, M22, M26). Allies are VOICES AND SCRIPTED BEATS, not followers (decision 2).
- **The Vael are people.** A Vael is not a monster in Campaign 2: M03 shows one in a cage, M10 fills a market with them, M11 is a palace of two factions, M22 is the loss of the ones who helped. The enemies are the Songkeepers (zealots who want the Grafting) and what they have made (Tollbearers, choirs, the Cantors).
- **The end** (`GAME_VISION.md`, provisional): the Root Bell can be silenced, not destroyed. Ines rings the counter-tone at M29 and closes the Gate from the Vael side, staying behind (M30, no combat: she walks the Descent back up in silence, sees the lamp, and does not take the last step). The foreshadowing is C1E4S01 The Quiet Room.
- **The lamp.** The title picture has a lamp on the quay; the Campaign 1 ending picture has her carrying it down the Gate's road; C2S02 is "Ines's Lamp". It becomes a game object: **the hand lantern** (E5), first as a borrowed one in M13, the real one in S02 (decision 3).

## The new things (engine steps, in the order the acts need them; each is pure or lazy, node-tested first, tried in the Range before a map depends on it; the cheap version is what I build first)
- **E1. THE QUIET FOLK (Act A, M03; at scale in M10).** A peaceful creature (`peaceful: true`): no attack, idle and wander, flees from shots and noise, never wakes the player's enemies, a corpse counts as an INNOCENT (`stats.innocents`: a message from the Tender, a rank penalty, never a death). The arc lamp's lock never picks one; a blast hurts. Cheap version: a rig, a definition, two sounds, a Range target; the bot never fires at one (a gate: no innocents harmed). NOT cheap: whether a crowd is readable at game scale (the dense market is the test).
- **E2. RESONATORS (M04).** A prop that RINGS when it hears a noise within its radius (the noise model exists: weapons have a loudness, `quiet` 14 m for the arc lamp): it sends the Cantors' expanding ring and lures what sleeps. Pure; the ring is existing machinery.
- **E3. CURRENT (M06).** A floor effect that pushes (a direction on the cell), for the player and the creatures; the same code as the drift floor's grip with a vector.
- **E4. THE CAVE KIT (M09 on; a node tool).** `Shape`-kit operations that carve organic rooms (blobs, bowls, tiers, fingers) with the same safety rules as the existing kit (never touch a door, never move a route; the fingerprints prove it). A cave is not a rectangle with bites: it is the answer to the shape contract in Act B.
- **E5. THE HAND LANTERN (M13).** A carried light with fuel: `p.lamp` lazy state; a lit lamp lights a radius in the view and WIDENS what creatures notice (a lit player is seen from 24 m, a dark one from 6 m; Hush-driven Tollbearers ignore light); oil pickups; Use or a key toggles it. The sim reads it (sight range), the view draws it (a point light at the camera inside the existing lamp budget). Ines's real lantern (S02) never burns out. The hardest piece of Act B's engine: it touches creature sight, the view's light budget, the HUD, the save format (lazy, migrated).
- **E6. CHIMES (M14).** A prop that sounds a note when it is used or shot; a trigger condition `seq:<id>` fires when a phrase is struck in order (a window of seconds); every note has a glyph and a colour as well as a pitch (a puzzle must not be an audio-only test). The solver is a node test (the same discipline as the Mirror Array).
- **E7. PULSE REFLECTION (M16, M24).** The expanding ring of the Cantors reflects off a wall (a second ring comes back from the stone): the cavern IS the attack pattern. Small, in `bossStep`'s ring code.
- **E8. CARRIERS (M19 cheap, M20 full).** A platform that moves SIDEWAYS on a pure clock (sectors only move up and down today), carrying whoever stands on it and what stands on it; the nav and the bot treat it like the Episode 3 platforms. NOT cheap: a floor that carries a player while they fight changes the collision order in `step`.
- **E9. THE NOISE TELL (M26; it can arrive earlier, with M04).** A HUD mark for how loud the weapon in hand is (quiet / loud) and a radius on the automap; render and shell only, the sim already has the numbers.
- **E10. THE COUNTER-TONE EMITTER (found M25, used M26 to M29).** The planned late alternate gadget (`GAME_VISION.md`): not a weapon in a slot but a held tool on its own key and pad button: it MUTES, in a cone, what is shielded by sound (a node's ring, a Reed, an Acolyte's ward, the Root Bell's phases) and stalls the Vael's own sound-based attacks. No ammunition, a cooldown and a charge like the arc lamp. It is the ending's tool.
- **E11. MULTI-PHASE BOSSES (M29, and the same machinery lightly in M16 and M24).** `phases` on a boss definition (thresholds of hit points that change its attack set and trigger arena actions through the existing trigger system).
- **No engine at all, by the existing kit:** the collapse of M27 (`sector` actions on `time:` and `enter`), the waves of M28 (`spawn` actions on `time:`), the politics of M11, the ambushes of M05, the flood of M12 (water cells and the arc's conduction), the loss in M22.

**New creatures (the owner's approval is asked for each before its rig and its bake: a creature is a definition, a rig, a recipe, a bake, sounds, a Range target and tests; the silhouette guard holds it under 0.5 overlap with every other kind):** the VAEL CIVILIAN (peaceful; M03), the ZEALOT (a Vael Songkeeper with a grafting lance: a hit that slows and drains, Act B, M09 lightly and M15 in force), the RESONANT (a fused-human hulk that pulses when it stands near others, M21), and three bosses: the SUB-CANTOR (M16), the HIVE CANTOR (M24), the ROOT BELL (M29). Sub-Cantor, Hive Cantor, Root Bell, plus one TBD": I propose to drop the TBD; M12's mass of the Drowned is the set-piece that takes its place). ## Rules carried over (no decision needed)
Gate 4: small batches the owner sets, each played before the next; agents never award COMPLETE; status is derived by `npm run validate`. The look rule and the shape contract above. **One new thing per map** (`introduces`, three beats, a secret on a main map: `tools/maplint.mjs`). **Roster variety is a gate this campaign** (the audit's finding): no creature kind above 35% of a map's placements after Act A's first two maps, and a new kind or a reskin in every batch. `tests/silhouettes.test.js` holds every new creature; `node tools/look/check-rigs.mjs` and `node tools/dev/measure-rig.mjs` fit its hit volume to the drawn body. ## Decisions for the owner (all can be overruled; my pick first)
1. **Peaceful Vael can be shot, with a consequence** (a message, a rank penalty, never a death) rather than being untargetable: an untargetable crowd is scenery, a shootable one is the M10 test. Alternative: props only.
2. **Allies are voices and scripted beats, not followers.** A follower is a whole system (pathing, targeting, health, saves) and the bot cannot rely on it; the loss in M22 works as a scripted scene.
3. **The hand lantern is a tool from M13, and Ines's own lamp (C2S02) is its upgrade.** It is the same lamp as the title picture's and the ending picture's. Alternative: no lantern, the dark of M13 solved by lit lanterns in the world.
4. **Six new creatures** as listed (three bosses, three kinds), each approved by you before its rig and bake. The vision's "plus one TBD" boss dropped.
5. **One engine tranche per act, in the Range first,** then map-only batches (the plan above), against engine per map.
6. **The ending:** Ines closes the Gate and stays (the vision's provisional line), shown after a silent walk (M30), told by `ENDINGS[2]` cards in the same voice as Campaign 1's. If you would rather she comes back, that is a card and a map's last room, and it is better to say so before M30 is written.
7. **Campaign 2 is thirty main maps** and, at a person's pace, many hours; the manifest asks for it and I have not cut it. Each act ends in a boss or a set-piece and is a natural place to stop and push.

## Risks and what is not known
- **Nothing here has been felt.** The neutral crowd, the lantern and the carriers are the three pieces I am least sure of as FEEL (a crowd you must not shoot, a light that costs something, a floor that moves under a fight); the Range yards exist so you can say so before a map depends on them.
- **The bot cannot judge them.** A bot that never fires on a peaceful Vael proves the crowd is passable, not that it is a fair or a good fight; the numbers are tuned against a bot that stands still and shoots; you play on Easy.
- **Evidence cost grows.** 68 maps and a ten-minute shared check: why one engine tranche per act.
- **Length.** Thirty maps is a campaign in itself; pace and pars are placeholders until a person has timed them.
- **The ending is the story's heart and the least certain text.** The cards for Campaign 1 were written in one sitting; Campaign 2's should be revised after the owner has played the Descent.
