## My own visual assessment (the same audit on my own game), 2026-10-13
Frames read: Gemini's 46 (current commit, SwiftShader); the Act B tours `review/level-c2m10..16` (generated 10-12, current gun); the creature sheets `review/assessment-2` (10-10, BEFORE the PT-035 coat changes); `review/pt-021/final` UI and Cradle frames (10-08); the Quay tour `review/level-c1e1m01` (2026-10-05, BEFORE the world pass and the new guns: used for sky and palette only). The harness refused my own capture job twice (`tools/dev/audit-capture.mjs`, written, never run), so NOTHING of Acts C and D has been seen except Gemini's three C2M30 frames.

Verdict: the look is a real look, and it is uneven. The skies, the ink-and-band treatment, the creature kit and the UI type are good and coherent. The ground, the light, the held gun and the boss staging are not at the same level, and the weakest frames are weak for the same reasons again and again.

Strong: the Quay dusk sky and its silhouettes (tower, crane, bollards); the Cradle Annex (vine walls, glowing pods); the catacomb walls of C2M13 (drawers with skull labels, lit gold); creature turnarounds (distinct hues and outlines: red coat, navy coat, white gown, brown brute, horn robe); the pause, intermission and death screens (serif caps, amber rules, plain tables); the HUD (readable in every frame).

Weak, ranked by how many frames it spoils:
1. FLOORS. One tone with sparse noise: Quay plaza and pier, C2M11 colonnade, C2M12 nave (a flat green-grey road to the horizon), Glass Steppe, the Annex water as scanlines. The walls carry the detail; the floor is a flat plane in most of the outdoor and corridor frames I read (I did not tally them).
2. THE HELD GUN IS NOT IN THE WORLD'S LIGHT. The cannon is the same full gold in a black catacomb, a cold blue nave, a green garden and a sunset. It pins the lower right quarter of every frame and breaks the mood of any dark one. (The stills tours start with the default cannon, so the variety of what a player holds is not judged here.)
3. LIGHT CLIPS INSTEAD OF GLOWING. C2M10: a lamp-lit pillar is one flat yellow, the figure beside it a featureless yellow-white cut-out; ten value bands and exposure 2.2 leave no mid-tone in a lit area.
4. DARK MAPS ARE BLACK, NOT DARK. C2M13: pure-black wall masses, a black cut-out creature, one unidentified red slab; only the one lit wall reads. No ambient floor, so the player cannot tell shapes in the dark.
5. THE SKY HAS CONFETTI. C2M11, C2M12, C2M14, C2M16: multicoloured pixel flecks over the dithered clouds read as noise in a still. Motion may change that; not judged.
6. BOSSES READ AS PROPS. Sub-Cantor (C2M16): a crimson mass filling the top of the frame, no face, limb or tell. Cantor (C1E1M08, 10-08 build, BEFORE the shield fix): teal streaks across the walls read as glitches; whether the fix cured that is not seen.
7. REPEATED MATERIALS AND PROPS: the Annex's vine panel on every face (wallpaper), the same stall twice on the Quay, six identical crate stacks at the Customs Hall.
8. HURT FLASH, and the title / key card text (see the first table).
Open, owner's taste: the default 480-pixel render width gives close walls and pillars chunky blotches (C2M10 pillar, Customs Hall door wall). It IS the look approved 2026-09-29; the question is whether the default should be 640.

Fix order (render only, no sim change; a Chrome job before and after each): (1) the hurt flash; (2) light the held gun by the place (the `lightbudget` / `LOOKS` lamp and hemisphere into the weapon scene); (3) a floor pass: lift the baked patches' contrast in `shading.js`, add per-look floor decals (planks, grates, puddles, rope, cracks, rubble) on the three exemplar maps first; (4) soften the lit-area clip (value bands over a brighter mid range, a lamp pool that falls off instead of cutting); (5) an ambient floor for dark maps (a rim of cool light on wall edges); (6) judge the sky flecks in motion; (7) the boss staging after (1) to (5), from real frames of each boss.

### Measured, not judged: the hurt flash (finding 1 is now a number)
`index.jsonl` records the post pass's `uDamage` at every hurt frame. 45 hurt frames over 25 maps: every one was a hit of 2 to 8 hit points (the biggest hit of the whole run was 8) and every one painted the screen at 0.52 to 1.00 of full red. Hits of 2 points (eight of them) sat at 0.56 to 1.00. Frames: C2M29 (the Root Bell, 2 hit points, 98 of 100 left): the whole 3D picture is red (the HUD text is drawn over it in its own colours, so the HUD is NOT washed; an earlier line of this note said it was) and the clapper cannot be told from the wall. C2M12 (3 points): the cathedral is a red wash. C2M22 (3 points): the walls are red. It does the most harm in the BOSS fight: the hit is small, the flash is total, and a shooting boss that hits often means the player spends the fight looking at red. This is the most certain thing in the audit and the cheapest fix (render only: the view scales the flash by the hit; do not touch `p.hurt`).

## The enemy models, looked at on the current build (2026-10-14, "are you happy with your enemy models?")
Evidence: `tools/look/shoot-rigs.mjs close` (all 26 creatures, 5 m, the dusk preset, through the game's real post pass; the largest bosses again at 14 m) and `poses` for the Tollbearer (idle, two walk phases, two tells, strike, dying, dead). Files `review/self-audit/enemies/`, `enemies-big/`, `poses-tollbearer/` (not committed). These are static frames of one rig at a time on a clean background: they judge shape, colour, silhouette and the Tollbearer's tell. They do NOT judge how any creature moves in a fight, how it sounds, or how it reads in a lit room at 480 px.

Verdict: the concept is the strongest thing in the game and about a third of the roster is as good as the game will get; the common grunts have no menace, the second campaign's climax creatures are props, and at melee range the best models turn to a few chunky pixels.

Tiers (my opinion, from these frames):
- Hero art, would ship as it is: Gate Warden (an arch of bone and brass with a bell and a ring), Cantor (a tall navy robe, branching crown, fork hands), Bellhand (a top hat, a raised bell), Sexton (a white gown with a spiked crown), Bulwark (a plated dome with a spear helm).
- Good: Tollbearer, Chorister, Zealot, Vael folk, Acolyte, Wardengraft.
- Characters that do not yet read as creatures: Hive Cantor (a pale figure hanging with arms out, a scarecrow), Sub-Cantor (a horn on a cone), the Root Bell (a bronze cage round a lantern: the final boss), Resonant (a three-headed drum), Weeping Mother (an organ-front crab), Gaunt and Gill (cute and thin, not threatening).
- Objects by design, each a different shape: Seal (an armillary), Root Anchor (a lamp on an X), Bell node (tines and a bell), Vat (a water tower), Reed (a bagpipe). They share no single "shoot this" cue.

What a hit game would change:
1. FACES. Every humanoid has a blank grey oval head with two teal dots. Nothing in the head says angry, awake or about to attack, and teal is on nearly everything, so the eyes carry no information. Give the grunts a face (brow, mouth) and make the eye colour the state tell (idle, hunting, striking). Render only, hit volumes untouched.
2. MENACE AND MASS. Tollbearer, Gaunt and Chorister are narrow figures in a mid value. Part of this is forced: the project's hit-volume width rule (`check-rigs.mjs`) keeps the upright 2.3 m figures in one column (see PT-035). A wider grunt is a combat change and the owner's call.
3. THE BELL IS THE WEAPON. The Tollbearer's tell is an arm raised straight up (good, readable in silhouette); the bell it is named for only shows a cyan glint. Make the bell swing and flare on the tell.
4. ONE CUE FOR EVERY NODE. One shared glow ring or colour for everything the player must shoot to break a shield.
5. THE CLIMAX CREATURES. The Hive Cantor, Sub-Cantor and Root Bell need a face or a gesture and a presence the size of the room. Design them first (the L0 look-bible tools: `tools/look/bake-concepts.mjs`), then build.
6. CLOSE RANGE. The models are drawn to read at 5 to 10 m; at 1 m in the game's 480-pixel render a brute is a flat blur with a block of pixels in its chest (C2M20, frame 15). Show 480 and 640 side by side before the owner decides the default.
7. MOTION IS UNSEEN. The Tollbearer's walk phases are nearly the same as its idle in these frames. Judging walk, windup and death needs a real fight in motion.
Nothing here is built or started.

## Measured afterwards (2026-10-14, node only: tools/dev/lumstats.mjs over all 581 gameplay frames) and the corrections it forces
The plan that follows from all of this is design/VISUAL_DIRECTION.md. The numbers, in one place, and the places they change what I said above:
- THE HELD GUN (confirmed, now hard): the cannon's own pixels (a rectangle fully inside it on the 29 tick-0 frames) have luma 109 to 116, sd 1.5, in every map, while the scene round it runs 26 to 167, sd 34.7: correlation 0.22. The gun is the same brightness in a black catacomb and a white dock. (My first attempt measured a rectangle that included background and showed a correlation of 0.71; that metric was contaminated and is withdrawn.)
- THE LIT-AREA CLIP (CORRECTED, narrower than I said): the share of the 3D picture at luma 230 or more is 2.1% on average; 12 of 581 frames pass 20% and 2 pass 35%. Exit frames average 14.6% against 1.4% for the rest. It is the lit exit chamber of every map and the finale (C2M28 plinth 39.7%, C2S02's lamp room 39.3%, the exits of C2M10, C2M09 and C2M11 near 30%), not lit areas in general. I ranked it second; it is a real but narrow problem, and the plan fixes it with a soft shoulder in the tone curve and not a change to the bands.
- BLACK IS NOT THE FLAW: 26% of the 3D picture is luma 25 or under on average (C2M26 45%, C1E2M05 43%, C2M13 43%, C2M29 42%). The Cradle Annex, the best frame in the set, is 43% black. A dark picture with a lit core is the look.
- THE COUNT: the capture covers 29 maps, not 34 as an earlier line of this note said: C1E1M01, C1E1M02, C1E1M08, C1E2M05, C2M02, C2M09 to C2M30, C2S01, C2S02.
- THE HURT FLASH AND THE HUD: an earlier line said the flash washes the HUD; it does not (corrected above). The 3D picture goes red; the HUD text keeps its colours.
