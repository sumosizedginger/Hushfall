## The engine steps (the builder's decisions; all can be overruled)

### E1. THE AIR STATE and LAUNCH VENTS (Batch A) (low gravity)
**Decision: no jump key.** The owner likes the movement as it is; the player has no vertical velocity today (the floor is `groundAt`, steps up to 0.6 m, drops any distance). Low gravity is a STATE the world puts the player in, not a button: a map entity `vent` (a plate in the floor, `{ type: 'vent', at, to: [x, z], time }`) throws a player who steps on it along a ballistic arc to the landing cell `to` (the arc is exact for the sim's integrator, the way the mortar and the Weeping Mother's tear are: `vy` from the gravity, the height difference and the flight `time`), in low gravity (`LOWG`, about 3 m/s^2: a 40 m flight hangs for 3 s).
- `p.air = { vy, t, gx, gz }` is lazy state: absent on the ground, so every world that has no vent is byte-for-byte what it was (proved by the route fingerprints on all runs). While it is set the player moves by `p.vx`, `p.vz` (carried: a little air control, a quarter of the ground acceleration), `p.y` is the arc, the weapon fires and the view looks as usual.
- Landing: the arc ends on the first tick where `p.y` is at or under `groundAt` (a hull plate, a ledge, a deck); a wall stops the horizontal part (the player slides down it); a `void` cell (new floor effect, `FX.v`: "open sky") is a MISS: the player takes 15 and is set back on the last solid cell they stood on (`p.lastSafe`), never killed outright, so a bad launch costs a few seconds and some health, not the level.
- Nav and analysis: `reach.js` and `traps.js` treat a vent as an edge from its cell to `to` (optimistic); `nav.js` creatures stay grounded (they do not follow through the air: a flyer already can); the route bot's `goto` BFS gets the same edge (the bot just walks onto the plate); `hitvolume`/enemy melee gating by `dyv` already handles a player at another height.
- View: the camera follows `p.y` (`view.eyeBase` already smooths stairs), a soft vignette and a held-breath sound while airborne, a long low whoosh at launch, a thud at landing.
- Falling LOADS (M01): a region on a clock `loads: [{ rect, period, warn, phase }]` (pure function of `w.time`, the same family as `breath.js`): a shadow grows on the deck and a creak builds for `warn` s, then a load lands on the rect: 30 damage to anyone standing in it, a thud, dust. The bot waits outside a warned load as it waits outside a spore bloom.
- Tests: `tests/air.test.js` (the arc lands where it says, from any starting height; no state without a vent; a void is a set-back not a death; determinism and save/load in the air), vent edges in reach/traps, loads on the clock, a route that crosses a vent in the node fingerprints.

### E2. INTERLOCKED AIRLOCK DOORS and VACUUM REGIONS (Batch C, after the swap)
- A door with `interlock: '<id of the partner>'` refuses Use (a prompt: "The airlock is cycling") while its partner is open or moving and for `cycle` s after the partner closes (pressure equalising): you cannot open both at once, which is what an airlock is. Breaching is a script action (`breach`), not a way in.
- A `vacuum: [{ rect, seals: [door ids] }]` region is in VACUUM while any of its seal doors (hull doors) is open: the player there takes 10 a second (a floor effect in all but name), the view frosts the edges of the screen and the score drops out (the atmosphere's silence), creatures do not care (they are grown for it). Closing the seal ends it; the interlock is what makes crossing the vacuum a decision: cycle the lock, cross in the time you have, cycle the next.
- Tests: the interlock rule, the vacuum damage and its end, the bot (its `use` op already waits at a refusing door, as at the sound-lock).

### E3. THE BEAM TRACER (Batch B, after the swap) (the first real puzzle; the plan's M04 section refines the puzzle: six mirrors, four receivers, latched doors)
- `src/engine/beams.js`, PURE: `traceBeams(map, mirrorStates)` walks each emitter's ray cell by cell in the four directions, turns at a mirror (`/` or `\`, set by the mirror's rotation 0 to 3), stops at a wall or a shut door, and lists the lit cells and the receivers reached. A mirror is a map entity `{ type: 'mirror', at, rot }` that Use turns a quarter step (`w.mirrors` lazy state; `mirror_turn` event); the beams are recomputed on a turn and on a door change; a receiver's state drives a door or a lift through the existing `do` actions (`when: 'lit:<id>'` joins the trigger conditions).
- A beam is light, not a weapon: it blocks nothing and hurts nobody (a puzzle you can lose is a worse one); the view draws it as a bright line with a soft halo and the receivers glow when lit.
- THE PUZZLE: the Mirror Array has an emitter, five mirrors (three on the lane, two decoys) and two receivers (the way on, and the secret exit's); a creature's shot never turns a mirror. The first mirror teaches (one turn does it), the last needs the order. A map message names the trick once and no more.
- The route bot needs nothing new: a canonical route presses `use` on each mirror the right number of times; a test traces the beams after the route's presses and requires every receiver lit (`tests/beams.test.js`: the tracer on hand-made grids, the loop-free rule, a mirror turn re-routes, the shipped array is solvable AND not solved at the start).

### E4. DRIFT (Batch D)
A floor effect `FX.d` ("drift": no friction worth the name: the player's velocity decays ten times slower and the acceleration is a third) for the zero-g arena of M05; with the vents of E1 it is "weightless" enough, with no third movement system. A drifting body also carries a shove farther: a hit, a blast and the tuning-fork's punt move creatures and props on it (the physics of `gravity.js` already does this for props).

### E5. THE GATE WARDEN (Batch E) (the one new creature)
A vast arch of grafted bone and bell-chitin standing in the mouth of the Gate with a ring of light round it (a silhouette of its own: no creature is a ring), about 1100 hit points, shielded (5%) while its three SEALS (nodes on the arch) stand. Its attacks: a SWEEP, a beam from its eye that turns through a half circle after a 3 s tell (pillars and walls stop it, mirrors in the arena turn it: a mirror that catches the sweep shatters a seal: the Mirror Array's lesson used as a weapon, and the way an unaided player can also kill the seals with guns); a ring pulse like the Cantors'; Drone-Gills called out of the Gate. The bot kills it conventionally (`killboss`, the seals first); the mirror is the player's elegant way, not a requirement. Rig, atlas (baked), sounds, a Range yard, tests as for every creature; silhouette guard.

### THE SHAPE CONTRACT: what "not just a square box" means here, in gates
What the shaping kit already did (Episodes 1 to 3): cut bays and corners into the walls. `node tools/dev/map-shape.mjs` (new, read-only, node only) measures what is left, over the 27 shipped maps: 10 contain a plain box room but none is mostly boxes (the most is 35% of a map's room area), the median room-fill is 0.81 (a notched rectangle). What the kit did NOT do, and that is what the owner's eye was catching: **a plan you can name** (no map is "a wheel" or "a spiral"; every map is rooms joined by corridors), and **a section that goes up and down**: 11 of the 27 have ONE floor height from end to end, 16 have one lid height (a flat floor under a flat ceiling is a box however its walls are cut), and only 7 have 30% or more of their floor off the commonest height (the median is 11%). The kit's S.hall / S.bays are still used, for dressing; they are not the idea.

Every Episode 4 map is therefore held to five axes, each written in its brief, each seen in the plan image and the stills:
1. **PLAN: one named figure** (a spiral, three islands, a wheel, a drum, a bubble, a funnel, a ring, an oval). The figure is the first thing the plan image says; the plan sentences below are the test: if the plan image does not read as that figure the map is not done. Round rooms use `Level.disc`; spirals, rings and tapers are written as loops over cells in the map source (a round ramp is a per-cell height from the angle: the format allows it, `L.ht` is plain data).
2. **SECTION: the floor goes somewhere and the lid is not flat.** At least four floor heights, a rise, a pit or a shaft; at least three room heights (a dome is a ceiling that follows the radius: per-cell ceilings are plain data too). The map format caps a height at 17.5 m (`z` in 0.5 m units) and the sim is 2.5D: ONE floor per cell, so a road cannot pass over itself; a climb is a spiral that opens outward (each turn lies outside the last) or landings that do not overlap.
3. **MASSING: one big thing in the middle of the space** that you walk round and that breaks every long sightline (the cable, the keel, the control spine, the lens-column, the great tree, the colossi, the void, the arch).
4. **LIGHT: a source you can point at** (the sun, a breach, an oculus, the white of the Gate) that makes shafts and hard shadow and says where up is.
5. **WAY ROUND: a loop or a second way** (a flank tunnel, a ring road, a vent shortcut) so a fight can be left, and a vertical link so a room is a place you come back through.
Measured now, enforced from Batch A's first map (a `shape:` block in the map lint, for Episode 4 and later only: it would fail the shipped maps): `boxRooms = 0`, `meanFill <= 0.80`, `heights >= 4`, `levelsPct >= 30` OR a void, `roofs >= 3` OR a void. The tool counts a void cell as closed, which flatters the sightline numbers of the open maps; the human check is the real-renderer stills, always.
