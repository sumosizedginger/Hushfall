## 0. What play taught, as laws for every remaining map (the other doc, Act D, obeys them too)
1. **FINDABILITY (the Ossuary winch, the owner: "it is impossible to find the switch").** The winch was a 0.5 m dark panel on a 6 m wide ring, in a dark map, found by a scan, named only by "find the winch on its wall". A thing that opens the way must be: (a) ON or BESIDE the lane, never off it; (b) LIT (a lamp or lantern prop within 6 m; in a dark map its own lamp now glows red); (c) NAMED by the objective by what it looks like and where; (d) RESTATED by a line at the foot of the sealed gate (an exit that is shut says what shuts it); (e) CHECKED BEFORE THE MAP SHIPS by a new node lint (section 7). One cheap addition for the shell (C0): the automap marks every lever, key door and exit once it has been SEEN (`mapview.mjs` already draws them for me; the player's automap does not).
2. **A mechanic is TOLD at the moment it matters** (the Warden's mirrors: the owner shot the seals and never turned a mirror). Every new piece has a line the first time it can hurt or help, on the lane, before the player has to guess.
3. **A state the player cannot read costs more than a hard fight** (the Cold Dock lock). Everything clocked in Act C runs on ONE visible beat (section 2); everything that is shut says why.
4. **The curve.** The owner liked "more and more enemies" and a difficulty that rises even on Easy. Act C rises to its peak in M21 and M24 and has one real trough (M22, the loss). Threat ratings 1 to 10 in the table below; the existing gates still hold (a perfect bot takes at least 10 damage on normal and 25 on hard, a passive runner never gets through, a fighter in invulnerability cannot walk through the exit).
5. **Gates are not all levers.** Act B was levers and a lock. Act C's ways on are: two relays on opposite banks (M17), kill-and-cut (M18), a key behind timing (M19), a hub reached only by riding (M20), three valves (M21), four latches then a held door (M22), a key and a door as in the Quay (M23), the boss (M24). Each one is told (law 1).
6. **One new thing per map, three objective beats, a secret on every main map, a roster no kind of which is above 35% (a boss map is exempt and says so), a look of its own, the SHAPE CONTRACT** (a figure you can name, at least four floor heights and three lid heights or a void, one big thing in the middle, a source of light you can point at, a way round). All unchanged.
7. **EVERY DAMAGE SOURCE HAS AN ANSWER THE PLAYER CAN FIND (PT-044, the owner on the Hive Cantor: "no way to avoid its damage").** A ring, a pulse, a front, a bloom, a beam: each has at least one stated answer on the ground where the fight happens (cover that blocks it, a height it passes under, a clock to wait out, a distance), the objective or a line says which, and a node test plays it against a player standing still (the model is the Hive Cantor's ring test in `tests/preview.test.js`). A hazard with no answer where the fight is is a defect, not difficulty.
8. **LIGHTS (PT-044, "lights hanging from the sky with no ceiling").** A lamp hangs only under a roof; in the open there is a post, a lantern or a brazier. The placement lint enforces it for every map and (now) every dev map.
9. **NO ONE-WAY PITS (PT-044, "no way to step back up").** A tier or ledge a player can step or fall down from has a step back up (0.5 m at most) somewhere within reach, or the floor is not a trap: the `no trap` lint, plus the half-step rule for tiered arenas (tested on every arena).
10. **COUNTS (PT-044, "overwhelming", twice).** A boss or set-piece map has at most about 20 hostiles awake at once: groups and waves wake in parts; the boss's adds are added only after the base fight has been judged. The dense crowds the owner likes ("more and more enemies") belong to the ordinary maps.
11. **COLOUR (PT-044, "extremely gray").** No map is one grey and no run of rooms is one material: a place has its own hue, rooms inside it differ in skin and light (the Unrung Gate's walk is the model), and a `LOOKS` row does not drop saturation below 0.6 except where the plan says so (C2M22's mourning halls, by design, with warm candles in it).

## 1. What the act says (the facts the text may use; to be merged into `design/LORE.md` as section 2c when the act is built)
- **The Hive is where the count is kept and sung.** It is not a creature and not a building: a district grown as an organ, with a throat (the gorge you approach by), nerves, a loom, a vein, a foundry and a heart. Everything in it works on one pulse, the **beat**, about every second and a half.
- **What it is for.** The deep stone of Anselm-Nine used to ring the whole world as one voice and has cooled. The Songkeepers believe that if the count is ever full the stone will ring again; the Hive sings the count in its place, with grafted voices, until it does. It has never been full. (LORE section 2: "the total is not enough, and a short count means they must graft more." The Hive is where that sum is done.)
- **Each map shows one organ and one fact:** M17 the count itself (a plate: owed, sung); M18 that the lattice is made of the grafted (a strand with a harbour badge in it; Port Marrow's number, plainly); M19 that Tollbearers are made at a rate (a tally: so many cradles a day, a ledger Ines can read as a count); M20 that voices are carried by rail to the Hive (a manifest: a car's run); M21 that a grafted voice can be fused with others into one that rings louder (the Resonant: say what it is once, plainly); M22 that the Silent who helped her are taken (lost on screen, never a fight); M23 that the Hive keeps copies of what the grafted remember, and has built Port Marrow from them, wrong; M24 that one voice conducts the lot (the Hive Cantor: "choir of one").
- **The Silent in M22** are the ones of the Undercantor Halls (M11): which wing the player went through there (`choice halls=silent` or `halls=songkeepers`) changes who stands in the cloister and what they say (section 5, M22).
- **The lamp.** In M23 the copy of the quay has a lamppost whose light is the one real thing in it: light is quiet (LORE). The secret C2S02 is that lamp; taking it makes the hand lantern eternal for Act D (a convenience, never a need: Act D can be played without it; its dark maps give oil as Act B did).
- **SIGNAL HOUSE speaks once in this act (M23), wrong,** as LORE says: the Hive copied the signal with the harbour. It is the only time it speaks before M30. The text says only facts the player can check against the Quay they played.
- Ines's own voice stays dry and about what she sees; no emotion named; she talks to a set that does not answer.

## 2. The Hive beat (a design device, the one new rule that crosses the act)
A constant `HIVE = { beat: 1.5 }` (seconds; a bar is four beats, 6 s). Every clocked thing in Act C runs on a multiple of it: breath gates, platforms, nerve pulses, shuttles, cars, the Resonant's pulse, the Hive Cantor's strokes. The sim only ever reads `w.time` (the same pure-clock rule as `breath.js`, `platforms.js`, `loads.js`), so saves, replays and bots need nothing stored. The player READS it: the lights of the Hive's own rooms (ribs, nerves, vats) swell on the beat (render only; below 1 Hz), and a soft low pulse sits under the ambience (audio only; the loudness is a setting-safe low level). The player learns it once in M17 (where it is only atmosphere, the first rib that swells and a line from HULL) and USES it from M18. In M24 the Hive Cantor is the beat; when it dies the beat stops and **Act D is quiet because of it.** That is the one structural echo between the acts, and it costs one constant and one light curve.

## 4. The two new creatures (the owner sees both in the Range before a map uses them)
- **THE RESONANT** (a fused-human hulk, `ENEMIES.resonant`). Three or four Tollbearers fused side by side at the hip into one wide, low body: a bar of shoulders, three heads at three heights, many arms, a ring of small bells welded round the waist that is what rings. Hit points 420, speed 1.1, height 2.0, hit width 1.0 (WIDE AND LOW: the silhouette guard's rule is "wide, low, tall or winged, not a fifth coat"; its bar-with-three-bumps outline is what keeps it under 0.5 against the Bulwark's dome). It slams (22, reach 2.4, a 0.9 s tell). **Its pulse:** on every beat, if at least one other hostile stands within 7 m, it sends a low ring (radius 14, `hitHeight` 1.0, damage 5 times (1 + neighbours) capped at 20): a Resonant in a crowd is a ring factory, a Resonant alone is a slow brute. So the verb of M21 is SEPARATION (a corridor, the harpoon, the fork's gravity, the line-thrower's blast, kill the small ones first); the ring passes over anyone more than a metre above or below it (a step is cover, as the Sub-Cantor taught). Sounds: a heavy chord on the beat, a groan. Rig `resonant` in `models_choir.js`, atlas `choir_resonant` (BAKED, red-brown and bronze).
- **THE HIVE CANTOR** (the act's boss, `ENEMIES.hivecantor`). A tall thin conductor, 4.6 m, HOVERING above the podium (a flyer: `hover`), cruciform (arms out), each long finger trailing a THREAD to a tier of the seats; the threads are drawn (render only) and snap when a tier's last singer dies. Its look is its own: a throat-pale figure with a crest of nerves, nothing like the Cantor's coat, the Grand Cantor's horn or the Sub-Cantor's bell robe. Hit points 1500. It never walks. Its attacks: the DOUBLE RETURNING RING (E7 exists: a ring that turns back at the wall, twice) on a bar's third beat; a CHORD every fourth bar (the three tiers' floors strike in order, inner to outer, on beats 1 2 3: a read-the-beat dance); from half hit points, or when the choir is dead, PHASE TWO: the beat is gone from the floors, a ring every bar, Choristers called from the dome (`summons`). The unison shield makes the choir the way in. Rig `hivecantor`, atlas `choir_hivecantor` (BAKED), eight sounds.
