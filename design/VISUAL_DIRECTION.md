## 1. What the game should look like (the direction, in seven rules)
The look approved on 2026-09-29 is a painted harbour-gothic nocturne seen through ink lines and value bands. I am keeping it. What is wrong is not the style but that the style is applied to surfaces and not to light. The seven rules every frame is held to:
1. **One light per frame.** A lit place has a bright core, a mid-tone fall-off and a dark edge. The top value band belongs to emissives (a lamp, a door of light, a muzzle flash), never to a wall or a floor.
2. **Value structure: floor under wall under sky.** The floor is the darkest large plane in an outdoor or hall frame and carries wear (gaps, cracks, wet, rubble). A creature is the second-brightest mass against it and has its own hue.
3. **Three materials a room.** A base, a trim, one accent. Not one skin on every face; not a checker to the horizon.
4. **The hand in the world.** The held gun is lit by the place it is in: dim in a catacomb, cold in a nave, warm at dusk. It never takes a quarter of the screen in a dark place.
5. **Enemies read by face and tell.** The eyes are the state: asleep, hunting, striking. The attack tell is a change of silhouette AND a change of the hot accent.
6. **Nodes and bosses are landmarks.** Everything the player must shoot to open a shield shares one cue. A boss is staged: backlit, large, with its reach drawn on the floor.
7. **The centre of the screen belongs to the fight.** HUD text, toasts and plates stay off the centre line.
Kept exactly as they are (the protect list): the ink outline and the banding, the skies, the creature palette and kit, the type, the HUD layout, the Cradle Annex as the target for a room, the Gate Warden, Cantor, Bellhand, Sexton and Bulwark as the standard for a creature.

## 2. What I measured (the evidence this plan stands on; `node tools/dev/lumstats.mjs review/self-audit/images review/self-audit/index.jsonl`)
- **The hurt flash.** 45 of 45 hurt frames over 25 maps flashed 0.52 to 1.00 for hits of 2 to 8 hit points. `hurtPlayer` (world.js:200) sets `p.hurt = 1` for any hit and the shader mixes 75% red (post.js:64). The `hurt` event already carries `amount` (the audio scales its gain by it, `src/audio/events.js:80`). It is the cheapest and most certain fix.
- **The held gun.** The cannon's own pixels (a core rectangle fully inside it on the tick-0 frames) have luma 109 to 116 (sd 1.5) in all 29 maps while the scene round it runs 26 to 167 (sd 34.7): correlation 0.22. The gun is the same brightness in a black catacomb and a white dock. Cause: `view.js:85-86` gives the weapon scene a FIXED hemisphere light (1.5) and key light (1.6); `setPlace(look)` (view.js:509) sets only the world's lights.
- **The clip is narrow, not general.** Mean top-band share (luma 230 or more) is 2.1% over 581 frames; only 12 frames pass 20% and 2 pass 35%. EXIT frames average 14.6% against 1.4% for the rest: the lit exit chamber of every map, and the finale (C2M28 plinth 39.7%, C2S02 lamp room 39.3%, the C2M10, C2M09, C2M11 exits near 30%). My earlier "the lit areas clip" was too broad; it is the exits and the ending.
- **Black is not the flaw.** 26% of the 3D picture is luma 25 or under on average (C2M26 45%, C1E2M05 43%, C2M13 43%); the Cradle Annex, the best frame, is 43% black. A dark picture with a lit core is the look. I withdrew "dark maps are black".
- **Frames I read:** about 90 of 637; the rest are listed as unread in the review doc and the numbers above cover all of them.

## 7. What can go wrong
- **A render change hides a bug in a map.** Every phase runs the frame-loop test over every shipped map and the dev maps, and every floor or wall swap is proved route-neutral by `route-fingerprint.mjs`.
- **The tone shoulder washes the dark maps.** The acceptance holds the black share within 3 points and every non-exit frame's mean within 5%.
- **A lit gun disappears in the dark.** The 0.35 floor and the lantern rim.
- **Faces fight the hit-volume rules.** A face is paint and a prim inside the rig; the fences are listed in V8 and run on every change.
- **The default 640 hurts frame rate on a weak GPU.** The owner plays on their own machine; the setting stays; the performance step of the Director's Plan (workstream H) owns the fallback.
- **I judge from stills of a bot's route.** The bot is not a person and a still is not motion: every V phase ends with the owner's own play, and their "it is still fun" outranks any number here.
- **A baked change needs a bake and a bake is slow under software GL.** `BAKE_PREVIEW=<dir> node tools/bake.mjs <ids>` bakes without touching `assets/baked`; one page per bake job.

### F1. The tuning fork: a creature that meets a wall is struck, it does not slide (S, NODE ONLY, DO FIRST; MAJOR, a defect met in play)
- **STATUS 2026-10-18: DONE in code and node tests** (`tests/fork-wall.test.js`, 5 tests; fingerprints 144 of 144 identical). Left: the Range probe J17 (a Chrome job, needs the owner's yes) and the owner's play. Props deliberately unchanged.
- **Cause.** `updateShoves` (`src/engine/gravity.js:219`) carries a punted creature with `tryMove`, which slides along a wall, and counts a wall impact only when the creature moved less than 40% of the distance it was asked to (line 228). A creature that meets a wall at an angle keeps most of its speed along the wall, so it is struck only at a corner or the next wall: exactly the owner's report. A head-on punt works, which is why the Range never showed it.
- **Fix.** Detect the wall by the BLOCKED COMPONENT of the motion, not by the total displacement: compare the intended step (dx, dz) with the step actually made; the part of the intended step along the contact normal is the blocked share n (0 to 1 of the speed). When n is 0.25 or more (a glancing scrape of about 15 degrees does not count, 30 degrees and over does) the creature is struck at once: damage = `impact` x the remaining speed fraction x max(0.35, n), so a head-on blow keeps today's full value and a glancing one does less but never nothing; the shove ENDS there (it stops against the wall, stunned, with the existing `shove_impact` thud), it does not slide on. The body-to-body case and the elite rule (an elite takes no impact) stay as they are.
- **Props to check in the same pass.** A thrown or punted prop moves by axis in `updatePhys` (gravity.js:77-78): the blocked axis bounces at 0.3 and the other keeps its speed, so a prop thrown at a wall at an angle also skates along it, and it breaks only when the blocked component is at least `breakSpeed` 14. That is probably right for a crate; the plan is to look at it after the creature fix and change it only if it reads wrong in the Range.
- **Tests** (`tests/weapons-pt22.test.js` has the punt tests): a creature punted at 20, 45 and 70 degrees into a long straight wall is struck on first contact and rests within 0.3 m of the wall; a straight punt is unchanged; an elite is not struck; damage is monotonic in the angle; no creature is ever moved through a wall. `node tools/dev/route-fingerprint.mjs check` must stay identical (the route bots do not carry the fork, so every run should be the same). Then the Range probe (J17).

### S1. The chainsaw's voice (M; audio)
- **Cause.** The source says so itself: the PT-022 sounds are "first guesses: not yet heard, not yet measured" (`src/audio/synth.js:718`), so the owner is the first person to hear them, and the design is wrong in three ways. (1) The engine is made of retriggered one-shots: `saw_rev` is a 0.5 s sawtooth at 95 Hz through a 650 Hz low-pass with a vibrato, a 190 Hz square and 1.5 kHz noise, started every `noiseEvery` = 0.4 s with a 0.05 s attack (`defs.js:264`, `saw.js:21`), so the loudness flutters at 2.5 times a second and there is no engine pulse in it. (2) `saw_hit` is the same 0.09 s noise burst and 160 to 200 Hz tone every `every` = 0.1 s: a machine gun, not a chain in a body. (3) Pitch never follows the spin or the load, so it cannot sound like an engine working.
- **Design: a sustained voice, not one-shots.** A new engine voice in `src/audio/engine.js`, beside the ambience layer, driven every frame by `p.saw.spin` (0 to 1), whether it is cutting and its heat. Layers: (a) a two-stroke pulse train at the firing rate, about 30 Hz at idle rising to about 105 Hz at full spin, from a narrow-pulse wave through a resonant band-pass that follows the rpm (300 to 900 Hz), with a random period jitter of about 6% and a slow burble at idle; (b) the chain: noise band-passed at 1.8 to 5 kHz, amplitude-modulated at the tooth-pass rate (70 to 130 Hz, tied to the rpm); (c) an exhaust body: low-passed noise; (d) load: while it cuts, the rpm sags by about 20%, the chain layer thickens and a wet grind is added from a set of four to six randomised chunks, no two alike, at the cut rate (flesh, not stone); (e) a heat stall: the rpm sags, it sputters, it stops. One-shots stay only for the pull cord (a ratchet of fast clicks slowing down, then the catch), the stop (the rpm falling away) and the plate kick.
- **Proof.** Objective checks added to `tools/dev/audio-qa.mjs` (no clicks at the loop seam, no loudness flutter under 8 Hz above a threshold, a spectral centroid and band energies in the range of a two-stroke engine), and then the owner's ears, which nothing replaces: three rendered WAVs (idle and rev alone, cutting, the whole pull-to-stall sequence) in `review/audio/` to listen to, iterated until they say it works. Each audio render is a Chrome job and waits for a named yes (J16). The other PT-022 sounds (the carbine, the line-thrower, the mortar, the fork) share the same "not yet heard" comment and are on the listening list of workstream G.
